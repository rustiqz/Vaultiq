Vaultiq testbed

Nothing here submits anywhere and nothing leaves the page — every form calls preventDefault. Serve it over HTTP rather than opening the file: file:// URLs have no hostname, so site matching cannot work, and Firefox gates content scripts on them separately.

python3 -m http.server 8000 from this directory, then save a login for localhost and work down. Sections 8 and 10 are expected to fail; they are here to keep the gaps visible.

1 · Ordinary sign-in

The base case. One username, one password.

Expect: clicking either field shows the picker with your saved logins. No password suggestion — you are recalling, not choosing. Submitting offers to save.

2 · Sign-up, with confirmation

Two password fields and no autocomplete hints. This is the shape most sign-up forms actually have.

Expect: Use a suggested password at the top of the picker, and choosing it fills both password boxes with the same value.

3 · Sign-up, declared properly

A single field marked autocomplete="new-password".

Expect: a suggestion, on the strength of the autocomplete attribute alone.

4 · Change password

Three password fields. The interesting one.

Expect: the suggestion fills new and confirm and leaves current untouched. If it overwrites current, that is a bug — the site would reject the change.

5 · Honeypot and disabled fields

A hidden field and a read-only one, which crawlers fill and people do not.

Expect: the username goes into Email, never into the hidden trap or the read-only box.

6 · Two forms on one page

Sign in on the left, register on the right — a very common layout.

Expect: each form is treated separately — a suggestion on the right only. Submitting either offers to save only that form's values.

7 · Rendered late

Appears two seconds after load, like a form behind a client-side router.

Expect: works normally. The picker attaches on focus, not on load.

8 · Submits by XHR, with no form event

A button handler rather than a real submission. Known limitation.

Expect: the picker works. The save prompt does not appear — there is no submit event to hear. Worth confirming so the gap is known rather than assumed.

9 · Inside an iframe

Same origin. The content script runs in every frame.

Expect: the picker works inside the frame, and anchors to the field rather than to the top of the page.

10 · Inside a shadow root

A web component. Known not to work.

Expect: nothing. Field lookup does not cross shadow boundaries. Confirming this tells us how much it matters.

11 · Checkout, declared properly

Every field named with its autocomplete token, the way a payment form should be. No password anywhere, so nothing here is site-scoped.

Expect: focusing any of these offers your cards — on this page as on any other, because a card belongs to no site. Picking one fills all five.

12 · Checkout, named the way developers actually name things

No autocomplete attributes at all. This is the guesswork path.

Expect: the first two are recognised and filled. The promo code is not — "code" alone is too ambiguous to act on, and guessing wrong would type a security code into a discount box.

13 · Billing and shipping in one form

Two sections, one form. The section prefix is what tells them apart.

Expect: filling from a billing field leaves every shipping field untouched, and the other way round.

14 · One-time code

The second step of a sign-in, on its own page as it usually is.

Expect: your authenticator accounts are offered, and picking one fills the code showing right now.
